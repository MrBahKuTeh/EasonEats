01 / All About Nick

Meet Nick DiGiovanni

A look at Nick’s journey, why I enjoy his food content, and his cookbook.

About Nick

His food journey

Born on 19 May 1996 in Providence, Rhode Island, Nick DiGiovanni is currently 30 years old. He developed a love for cooking when he was still a kid, inspired by watching his grandmother and great-grandmother prepare food. Interestingly, Nick went on to study at Harvard University, where he designed his own area of study called Food and Climate. For me, it’s pretty cool to see how his passion for food goes beyond just cooking, as he also explored the relationship between food and the environment. It shows that there is so much more to food than simply making something delicious!

Nick’s journey took a big step forward when he competed in Season 10 of MasterChef, where he became the youngest-ever finalist in the show’s history and finished in third place. He also gained hands-on experience in professional kitchens, including Benu in San Francisco and Waypoint in Cambridge. What I find interesting about Nick is how he combines his professional cooking experience with a fun and creative approach to food. Instead of making cooking seem complicated or intimidating, he presents it in a way that feels exciting and accessible to everyday food lovers.

Today, Nick is widely known for his entertaining cooking videos, creative food experiments and impressive food-related world records. He is also the author of Knife Drop: Creative Recipes Anyone Can Cook, which encourages people to try making delicious dishes at home. Personally, I like how his content makes food more than just something we eat — it turns cooking into an experience filled with creativity, curiosity and fun. That’s what makes Nick DiGiovanni an interesting celebrity chef for me to feature on EasonEats!

02 / Personal choice

Why Nick Is My Favourite Chef

One of the main reasons why Nick DiGiovanni is my favourite chef is the type of content he creates. What I enjoy most is that his videos are not always just about himself or his own cooking. He often collaborates with different celebrity chefs and food creators from around the world, giving viewers the chance to explore different cooking styles, techniques and personalities. As someone who enjoys watching food content, I find this really interesting because I get to discover other chefs that I might not have known about before. It is almost like getting to know several chefs through one channel! I also enjoy seeing how each chef approaches the same ingredient or dish differently, as it reminds me that cooking is not just about following a recipe but also about creativity, experience and personal style.

Another thing that makes Nick’s journey exciting to follow is his ambition to surpass Gordon Ramsay in YouTube subscribers. When I first learned about this goal, I was curious to see whether he could actually achieve something that seemed so challenging, especially when Gordon Ramsay is already such a well-known chef worldwide. What makes this interesting to me is that Nick did not simply rely on his reputation as a MasterChef finalist. Instead, he continued creating entertaining food videos, experimenting with unusual ideas and collaborating with well-known personalities. In fact, Nick has since surpassed Gordon Ramsay in YouTube subscribers, making his ambition even more fascinating to look back on (Youshaei, 2025). His journey makes me wonder what other goals he will set for himself next, and that curiosity is one of the reasons I continue to follow his content.

Besides his cooking skills and ambition, I also like how Nick makes food content entertaining and approachable. His videos can be creative, funny and sometimes surprisingly adventurous, which makes me want to keep watching even when I am not looking for a particular recipe. I appreciate how he can introduce cooking techniques and food knowledge without making the experience feel like a serious cooking lesson. To me, that is what makes Nick stand out: he combines his skills as a chef with the creativity of a content creator, making food enjoyable for both experienced cooks and ordinary food lovers. Overall, Nick DiGiovanni is my favourite chef because his content allows me to discover other chefs, learn about different cooking styles and follow an ambitious creator whose food journey keeps me curious about what comes next!

03 / Cookbook

Knife Drop: Creative Recipes Anyone Can Cook (2023)

Released on 13 June 2023, Knife Drop: Creative Recipes Anyone Can Cook was Nick DiGiovanni’s very first cookbook. After sharing his cooking knowledge through online videos for years, Nick wanted to bring his favourite recipes, personal experiences and cooking tips together in one book. What I find interesting is that he didn’t write it just for professional chefs or experienced cooks. Instead, he wanted to make cooking fun and accessible to everyone, even those who are just starting out in the kitchen. With creative recipes, practical tips and QR codes linking to cooking tutorials, Nick encourages readers to be more confident, experiment with food and enjoy their own cooking adventures!

What is a foodie?

Food is more than just eating

To me, being a foodie is about more than just eating because I’m hungry. It’s about enjoying the whole experience of food — discovering new places, trying different dishes, exploring different flavours and appreciating the little details that make each meal special. I am a foodie because I enjoy seeking out new restaurants, comparing different dishes, learning about the stories and cultures behind the food and sharing my honest opinions with others. Whether I’m enjoying Malaysian food, grabbing a pizza with friends or trying a new burger spot, I love capturing those moments and sharing my experiences through photos, reviews and recommendations. I also enjoy thinking about what makes each meal memorable, from the taste and presentation to the atmosphere and the people I share it with. For me, food is a way to explore, create memories and discover something new with every bite. And that’s what EasonEats is all about — my personal food adventures, one delicious bite at a time!

Post 01 · Malaysian food

#1. Ayam Gepuk from Gepuklah — So Good, But That Queue!

Some food is just so good that you keep thinking about it even after you’ve finished eating, and Gepuklah’s Ayam Gepuk is definitely one of those meals for me! The star of the show is the fried chicken, with its golden, crispy exterior and tender, juicy meat inside. But let’s talk about the sambal, because that’s where things get serious! As someone who genuinely loves spicy food and has a pretty high spice tolerance, I’d rate the sambal a 7/10 for spiciness. Honestly, it might be too intense for people who aren’t used to spicy food, but for me, it was still acceptable and added a fiery kick that made every bite more exciting. Combined with the rice and side dishes, the whole meal was savoury, satisfying and seriously delicious. If Gepuklah were closer to my place, I could easily see myself eating this once or even twice a week. Yes, I enjoyed it THAT much!

Price-wise, I think it’s pretty reasonable and fair for what you get. When a meal tastes this good and leaves you feeling satisfied, I don’t mind paying for it. However, there are two things that make me hesitate before visiting again. First, the location is quite far from where I live, so getting there takes more effort than I’d like. Second, the queue can be disgustingly long! I guess that’s what happens when a place becomes this popular, but waiting ages when you’re already hungry is definitely not the most enjoyable part of the experience. The food makes me want to return, but the distance and waiting time make me think twice.

Still, if you ask me whether I’d eat Gepuklah again, my answer is absolutely YES! The crispy chicken, fiery sambal and reasonable price make it worth considering despite the long queue. I just wish it were nearer to my place and that I didn’t have to wait so long to get my hands on it. For now, Gepuklah remains one of those places I crave whenever I’m in the mood for seriously good Ayam Gepuk. Great food, fair price, painful queue — but honestly, that sambal is hard to forget!

EasonEats rates it: 9/10 🌶️🍗

Post 02 · Pizza

#2. Pizza Mansion — My Go-To Spot for Special Occasions

Some places are just worth coming back to, and Pizza Mansion is definitely one of them! I’ve visited this place multiple times, especially when there’s a special occasion or something worth celebrating. Whether it’s a birthday, a gathering with friends or simply an excuse to enjoy some good food, Pizza Mansion always comes to mind. The first time I visited was at their KL branch, and later, I tried their Petaling Jaya branch, which is much closer to my place. They have three branches across the Klang Valley, so there are a few locations to choose from depending on where you are. What I appreciate most is that they offer an online booking system, which makes planning a meal much easier. I usually don’t have to wait long when I book ahead, and honestly, that’s a huge plus when all I want to do is sit down and enjoy my food!

Now, let’s talk about my favourites, because these two dishes deserve their own spotlight: Silence of the Lambs and Truffle Mac & Cheese! First up is the Silence of the Lambs pizza. The name itself is already memorable, but the flavour is what keeps me coming back. It features Moroccan lamb meatballs on a tomato-chilli base, topped with melted cheese, green chillies, yoghurt, harissa and coriander. I love how the rich, savoury lamb pairs with the spicy tomato base, while the yoghurt adds a creamy, cooling contrast to the bolder flavours. Every bite brings together a mix of savoury, spicy and tangy notes, making it a really satisfying pizza. And then there’s the Truffle Mac & Cheese — honestly, if you’re a cheese lover, this one is hard to ignore! The macaroni is coated in a rich, creamy blend of cheeses, with truffle adding that distinctive earthy aroma and deep, savoury flavour. It’s comforting, cheesy and indulgent, the kind of dish that makes you want just one more bite even when you’re already full.

Price-wise, I think Pizza Mansion is pretty reasonable and fair considering the quality of the food they serve. For me, it’s not just about paying for a pizza; it’s about enjoying well-made food with flavours that stand out. Of course, it might not be somewhere I visit every week, but that’s mainly because I tend to save it for special occasions rather than because of the price. The combination of quality food, creative menu choices and the convenience of booking ahead makes it a place I’m happy to revisit. If you’re looking for somewhere to celebrate with friends or enjoy a proper pizza night, I’d definitely recommend giving Pizza Mansion a try. Just make sure you leave some room for the Truffle Mac & Cheese — trust me, you don’t want to miss it!

EasonEats rates it: 9/10 🍕🧀

Post 03 · Fast food

#3. Shake Shack @ Sunway Pyramid — Is It Really Worth the Hype?

I finally got to try Shake Shack at Sunway Pyramid, and honestly, I have mixed feelings about this one. Shake Shack is an American burger brand that has become popular worldwide, so I was curious to see whether its burgers would really stand out from the usual fast-food options we have in Malaysia. After trying it for myself, I’d say the food was okay — nothing terrible, but nothing that completely blew me away either. The burger had the familiar combination of a juicy beef patty, melted cheese, fresh vegetables and sauce, while the fries were pretty standard crinkle-cut fries. Everything was enjoyable enough, but I didn’t walk away thinking, “Wow, I need to come back for this!” Maybe my expectations were a little too high because of all the hype surrounding the brand.

Now, let’s talk about the price, because this is where things get a little difficult for me. I understand that Shake Shack originated in the United States and operates as an international brand, so there may be additional costs involved. However, that doesn’t automatically make the price feel worth it to me as a customer. For a burger-and-fries meal, I personally find it expensive, especially when I compare it with more familiar options like McDonald’s. At the end of the day, both places serve burgers, fries and other fast-food favourites. Although the ingredients, preparation and flavours may differ, I didn’t feel that the taste difference was big enough to justify paying so much more. If I’m craving a burger, I’d honestly rather spend less at McDonald’s and still enjoy a satisfying meal. For me, the issue isn’t that Shake Shack serves bad food; it’s that the overall experience didn’t feel special enough for the price.

Another thing worth mentioning is that Shake Shack has several branches across Malaysia, including Sunway Pyramid, Pavilion Kuala Lumpur, The Exchange TRX and KLIA2, making it accessible to people in different locations. However, having more branches doesn’t necessarily mean I’ll be rushing back for another visit. I’ve only tried the Sunway Pyramid branch once, and, to be honest, I don’t really see myself revisiting it anytime soon. I’m glad I tried it because at least now I know what the hype is about, but I wouldn’t go out of my way to have it again. If I’m craving a quick burger, there are other options that suit my budget better.

Overall, I’d say Shake Shack is worth trying once if you’re curious about the brand, but it personally didn’t win me over. The food was decent, the experience was fine, and I can understand why people might enjoy it. However, when I consider the price, flavour and other fast-food alternatives available, I just don’t think it offers enough value for me. Maybe it’s a matter of personal preference, but I’d rather save my money for food that leaves a stronger impression on me. No hate to Shake Shack — it simply wasn’t my cup of tea, or should I say, my kind of burger!

EasonEats rates it: 5/10 🍔
